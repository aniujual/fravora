Fravora ("we," "our," or "the App") is committed to protecting your privacy. This Privacy Policy explains what information is collected, how it is used, and what choices you have regarding your data. Fravora contains no advertising. The App does not use or collect the Android advertising ID.

Claudiu-Iulian Bumbel, trading as aniujual software, is the data controller for Fravora. You can contact us at aniujual.dev@yahoo.com or at Gardony Geza 6/8, 307160 Dumbravita, Romania.

2. Information We Collect

2.1 Photos and Personal Contact Details

Fravora does not require your name or contact details to use the App. Your photos, videos, and media files are not uploaded to Fravora's own servers.

2.2 App Usage Statistics (Firebase Analytics)

With your consent, Fravora uses Google Firebase Analytics to collect pseudonymous information about how the App is used. This may include:

  • Device type, manufacturer, and operating system version
  • App version
  • Country and language settings
  • App session duration and frequency
  • Feature usage patterns
  • A random app-instance identifier

This data is pseudonymous and linked to a random identifier, not your name or contact details. It is used to understand how the App is used and decide what to improve, not to identify you or build a profile about you. Google also processes technical data such as your IP address when it is transmitted.

2.3 Crash Reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

With your consent, Fravora uses Firebase Crashlytics to collect crash reports, which may include stack traces, device model, operating system version, app version, app state at the time of the crash, and a random installation identifier. Although the App filters technical error details, reports may in rare cases still contain them. Firebase collects fatal crash reports in their original form. Crash reports are collected only with your consent.

2.4 Website Analytics

When you visit fravora.app, we use Google Analytics, provided through a separate Firebase project for this website, to understand aggregate website use. Google Analytics may collect information such as pages viewed, approximate location derived from your IP address, device and browser type, referral source, and session duration. We use this information to maintain and improve the website. Website analytics is kept separate from the App's Firebase Analytics data. We ask for your choice before enabling website analytics; you can change it at any time using the Analytics Preferences link in the site footer.

2.5 Location Data

Weather is optional and off by default on new installations. Users who enabled it earlier keep that setting. When weather is on, Fravora uses your device location if location permission is granted; otherwise, it obtains an approximate location from your IP address through ipwho.is, which receives your IP address. Coordinates are sent to Open-Meteo and MET Norway (api.met.no) for forecasts, and city searches are sent to Open-Meteo's geocoding service. Fravora does not store your location. No weather request happens while the setting is off. See Section 4.8 for these services.

2.6 Voice Data

If you use the voice control feature (Pro tier), voice processing is handled in two stages:

  • Wake phrase detection is processed entirely on your device using the OpenWakeWord library. No audio data is transmitted externally during wake phrase detection.
  • Command recognition uses Google's Speech Recognition service to interpret your spoken commands after the wake phrase is detected. Audio captured during the command listening window is transmitted to Google's servers for processing. This audio is subject to Google's Privacy Policy.

Fravora does not store, log, or retain any audio recordings or transcripts of your voice commands. Voice control is an optional feature and can be disabled at any time from the App's settings.

2.7 Photo Metadata

Fravora reads EXIF metadata on your device, including photo GPS coordinates and date taken, when preparing and caching photos. This can happen even when the metadata overlay is disabled. The metadata overlay (Pro tier) uses this information to display photo details during the slideshow.

When the Pro metadata overlay and its location or landmark option are enabled, the active slideshow can use available photo GPS coordinates to obtain locality and nearby-landmark labels. Android Geocoder may receive unrounded photo coordinates or a nearby search area. GeoNames locality data is bundled with the App and used offline. If Enhanced landmark lookup is enabled, Fravora may additionally query Overpass (including its Kumi mirror), Nominatim, Geoapify when configured, Wikipedia, and Wikidata. Wikipedia normally uses a snapped search cell, but a limited targeted follow-up can use photo coordinates rounded to four decimal places; Nominatim receives coordinates rounded to four decimal places, Overpass uses a snapped search-area center, and Geoapify uses the search-area center rounded to five decimal places. Wikidata requests use place or entity IDs obtained from earlier results. The photo image itself is not sent by these geocoding requests. This also applies to Google Photos items if their downloaded metadata contains GPS coordinates. Photo GPS is separate from your device's current location and does not require Android device-location permission. See Section 4.9 for providers and Section 5.4 for local storage.

2.8 Issue Reports

If you use the in-app issue reporting feature, Fravora opens your email app with a message to our support address. The option to include diagnostic logs is selected by default; you can untick it before preparing the email. Logs can contain filenames, file paths, photo dates, location labels and photo GPS coordinates. Review any logs before sharing them. Fravora collects an attachment from its own process logs only when you choose to prepare a report with logs included; it does not automatically send the report. Your chosen email service processes the message and attachment when you send it.

Information you send to support is shared voluntarily and is used solely to investigate and resolve the reported issue. We do not forward your report to third parties. Exported log files are kept in the App's local cache, with only the three most recent retained when a new export is prepared; Android may also remove cached files. Support-email retention is described in Section 2.11.

2.9 Your Consent Choices

Before onboarding on first launch, the App shows a consent dialog with two separate optional toggles, both off by default: App usage statistics (Firebase Analytics) and Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics). Accept All, Decline All, and Save Choices are presented with equal prominence. Nothing is collected for these purposes until you choose, or if you decline, and the App works the same either way. Nothing Firebase-related runs at App startup before you answer the consent dialog. The configuration file request described in Section 2.12 happens before you answer and regardless of your consent choice. You can change or withdraw your choice at any time in Settings > Support. Turning off usage statistics stops collection and resets analytics data on your device; turning off crash reports stops collection and deletes unsent reports. Data already sent is kept until the retention periods in Section 2.11 end. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing that took place before you withdrew consent.

2.10 International Data Transfers

Google (including Firebase, its favicon service, and Android geocoding services where used), GitHub Pages, ipwho.is, Open-Meteo (including its geocoding service), MET Norway, OpenStreetMap-based services (including Overpass API and Nominatim), Geoapify when configured, Wikimedia Foundation (Wikipedia and Wikidata), radio-browser.info, radio streaming providers, and Amazon Appstore are third parties we do not control. Some may process data outside the EEA or UK. Their privacy policies govern their processing, as described in Section 4. Where required, Firebase transfers rely on safeguards such as applicable data privacy frameworks and standard contractual clauses. You can withdraw your consent for analytics and crash reports at any time using Settings > Support; the configuration file request does not depend on that consent.

2.11 Retention

  • Firebase Analytics event data is retained for 2 months; user-level data, including identifiers and user properties, is retained for up to 14 months.
  • Firebase Crashlytics keeps crash stack traces, extracted minidump data, and associated identifiers (including Crashlytics Installation UUIDs and Firebase installation IDs) for 90 days before starting the process of removing it from live and backup systems.
  • Support emails are deleted once a year, at the end of December.
  • Photo metadata and geographic lookup results are stored locally as described in Section 5.4. Geographic cache freshness periods are not guaranteed deletion deadlines. Exported diagnostic logs are retained as described in Section 2.8.
  • Your App analytics and crash-report consent choice is stored locally on your device until app data is cleared or the App is uninstalled.

2.12 Configuration File

At startup, even before you answer the consent dialog and regardless of your answer, the App downloads https://fravora.app/remote-config.json. This small static file contains safety and configuration switches, such as disabling a feature that is malfunctioning. The request carries your IP address and standard HTTP request details: it sends only an ETag header and the default Android HTTP user agent, with no identifier. The website and this file are hosted on GitHub Pages, which receives IP addresses and standard request data. See GitHub's Privacy Statement. Our legal basis is our legitimate interest in keeping the App working and safe, GDPR Article 6(1)(f). This request is separate from consent-based analytics and crash reports.

4.3 Cloud Storage Providers (optional, user-initiated)

Current cloud sources are Google Photos, Microsoft OneDrive, Dropbox, and Immich. If you choose to connect a cloud source, Fravora uses provider-appropriate authorization (Google or Microsoft OAuth, Dropbox OAuth, or server URL + API key for Immich). The App's media browsing and display are based on the items, folders, or albums you select.

  • Your photos are downloaded to your device solely for display in the slideshow
  • Photos are cached locally on your device at screen resolution to improve performance
  • Cloud authorization credentials are stored securely on your device. Provider-specific authorization values may be relayed temporarily through Firebase Firestore during TV pairing, as described in Section 5.5
  • Selected cloud media is accessed and cached for slideshow display and associated metadata overlays. Photo metadata may be read and used for geographic lookups as described in Sections 2.7 and 4.9; we do not use your media for advertising or profiling
  • You can disconnect a cloud account in the App or revoke access through the provider

Google Photos uses short-lived Google access tokens; Fravora does not request or store Google refresh tokens. During TV pairing, a short-lived Google access token is temporarily relayed through Firebase Firestore as described in Section 5.5.

4.4 Google Photos API (additional disclosure)

When you connect Google Photos, Fravora uses the Google Photos Picker. You choose photos and videos in Google's own picker, and Fravora receives access only to the items you select, not to the rest of your library. Selected items are downloaded to your device for display. Specifically:

  • We access only the media items you select
  • Selected items are downloaded, resized to fit your device's display resolution, and stored in a dedicated cache on your device, together with basic details such as date, location and orientation read from the photo. They stay there until you remove the item or the source, or until the app clears the oldest items automatically when your device is critically low on storage. You choose how much free space the app must always leave on your device.
  • We do not modify, delete, or upload any content to your Google Photos library
  • We do not upload your selected photo images to geographic lookup services. If a downloaded item's metadata contains GPS coordinates and you enable the relevant overlays, those coordinates can be processed by Android Geocoder and, when Enhanced landmark lookup is enabled, the conditional providers described in Section 4.9
  • We use your Google Photos data only for slideshow display and its associated metadata features, not for analytics, advertising or profiling. Diagnostic logs you choose to send to support may include photo details as described in Section 2.8
  • Temporary Google Photos image-download URLs may be cached in memory for a connection and media item. They are not persisted to disk, and a URL may be reused from that in-memory cache while available

During Google Photos TV pairing, Fravora requests the following Google authorization scopes:

  • profile, email, and openid (Google sign-in): standard OpenID Connect scopes that authorize access to basic Google account identity information. If the downloaded media contains GPS metadata and you enable the relevant overlays, photo coordinates can be sent to geographic lookup providers as described in Section 4.9. Provider-specific authorization values may also be temporarily relayed during TV pairing as described in Section 5.5. If you choose to send diagnostic logs to support, those logs can include photo details as described in Section 2.8.

4.9 Photo Reverse Geocoding

Photo-location and nearby-landmark lookup uses available photo GPS metadata during the active slideshow window, not bulk geocoding of your library. Lookup requires Pro photo metadata to be enabled and either location or landmark display to be enabled. Android Geocoder may receive photo coordinates or a nearby or derived search area. GeoNames locality data is bundled with the App and used entirely offline. Enhanced landmark lookup may additionally query the following external geographic services; the photo image itself is not sent by these requests:

  • Overpass API: receives geographic search queries for nearby OpenStreetMap features around a snapped search-area center, including through its Kumi mirror. Each service's operator controls its own processing.
  • Wikipedia (Wikimedia Foundation): receives geographic search queries for nearby landmarks. Searches normally use a snapped search cell, but a limited targeted follow-up can use photo coordinates rounded to four decimal places. See the Wikimedia Privacy Policy.
  • Geoapify: may receive geographic search queries (using a search-area center rounded to five decimal places) when configured in the App build. See Geoapify's Privacy Policy.
  • Nominatim: receives geographic queries (with coordinates rounded to four decimal places) for reverse geocoding of photo locations. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation Privacy Policy and Nominatim Usage Policy.
  • Wikidata (Wikimedia Foundation): receives place or entity IDs obtained from earlier results rather than photo coordinates. See the Wikimedia Privacy Policy.

Geographic queries may disclose exact or near-exact photo coordinates or a derived search area, and online services also receive your network IP address as part of the connection. These lookups can use GPS metadata from any supported photo source, including Google Photos when available. They do not require Android device-location permission. Results are cached locally as described in Section 5.4. For Fravora, the legal basis is providing the metadata display feature you choose (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)); online providers may process request and connection data under their own privacy terms and retention practices. Controls are described in Section 6.5.

5. Data Storage and Security

5.1 Local Storage

All app configuration, including your selected photo sources, album settings, scheduling preferences, overlay settings, and all other app preferences, is stored locally on your device in private app storage accessible only to Fravora.

5.2 Credential Security

Credentials for network shares (SMB), Immich API keys, and cloud authorization credentials are stored on your device using Android Keystore-backed EncryptedSharedPreferences when available (with a fallback to private, app-only storage if encrypted storage cannot be initialized on the device). If you use the optional local-network QR or manual transfer helper to send Immich credentials from your phone or browser to your TV, or if you connect to an HTTP Immich server, a local SMB/NAS share, or an HTTP radio stream, that traffic is sent over your local network or configured connection without TLS encryption. Google Photos uses short-lived Google access tokens, and Fravora does not request or store Google refresh tokens. During TV account pairing, some provider-specific authorization values are temporarily relayed through Firebase Firestore over HTTPS; see Section 5.5. Fravora does not operate its own server that receives or stores your photos.

5.3 Photo Cache

Photos from all sources (local, network, and cloud) are cached locally on your device, sized to fit your device's display resolution, to improve slideshow performance. The size of the general image cache is set in the App's settings (128 MB to 2048 MB). When the limit is reached, the oldest cached items are removed automatically. Photos picked from Google Photos are kept in a separate cache limited by a minimum free-space setting that you choose. If your device runs low on storage, the App pauses downloads and may remove the oldest cached items automatically. Cached photos are stored in private app storage and are not accessible to other apps.

5.4 Photo Metadata

Photo metadata, including available EXIF GPS coordinates, capture dates and resolved location or landmark labels, can remain in local media-cache sidecar files and bounded local databases and caches. Metadata extraction can occur while preparing and caching media even when the overlay is off; geographic lookups are separately controlled as described in Section 4.9.

Geocoding and lookup caches have their own size limits and expiry rules. Cache freshness or expiry does not establish a universal deletion deadline for all stored photo metadata. Turning off metadata, location or landmark display does not erase existing sidecar files or cached results. Local data can be removed by clearing the App's storage through Android settings or uninstalling the App. Removing local data does not delete request records or logs already held by geographic service providers; those providers control their own retention.

5.5 No Fravora-Operated Photo Servers

Fravora does not operate its own server that receives or stores your photos. During TV pairing, the App on the TV or device initiating pairing signs into Firebase Authentication anonymously so it can create, own, and delete its pairing documents; that anonymous device account is reused across pairing sessions on that device rather than deleted after each session, and only replaced if its token becomes invalid. When you open a valid TV pairing link in your browser, Firebase Authentication also automatically signs in anonymously before you press a button, solely so the page can read the pairing record and relay the selected provider result. Firebase creates random user IDs that are not linked to your identity. On the pairing web page, Firebase Auth's default local persistence keeps the sign-in in that browser during the flow; when the browser flow reaches a terminal outcome (including success, denial, expiry, or an already-used code), the page attempts to delete its temporary anonymous Auth account, which also clears its browser sign-in. If deletion fails, the page attempts to sign out. Any anonymous Auth accounts (from the pairing page or inactive devices) that remain are manually deleted at least once a year. The standalone page does not show the website analytics-consent choice; anonymous sign-in is limited to the pairing flow and does not enable website analytics. During pairing, Firestore has a limited, temporary role as a relay: for Google Photos, a short-lived Google access token is relayed through a temporary pairing document; for Dropbox, only an authorization code is relayed, not a Dropbox access or refresh token. The PKCE code verifier remains on your device. Pairing documents are deleted by the TV as soon as the token or code arrives, or if pairing is cancelled or times out. Security rules accept a token only within 15 minutes of the document's creation, and a Google access token expires in about an hour. Access to a pairing document requires its random 12-character code. If the TV app is closed or uninstalled while pairing is pending, the document may remain in Firestore. The app removes leftover documents the next time it starts (unless the app was uninstalled mid-pairing, which removes its local ledger), and we delete any remaining ones at least once a year. A leftover document can contain only an access token that has already expired or an unusable authorization code. It never contains a refresh token, your email, or any of your files. Firestore is not used to store photos or long-term account credentials. See Section 4 for the third-party services used by Fravora.

6. Your Rights and Choices

6.1 Analytics and Crash-Report Consent

You can change or withdraw your consent for App usage statistics and crash reports at any time in Settings > Support. Turning off usage statistics stops collection and resets analytics data on your device. Turning off crash reports stops collection and deletes unsent reports. Data already sent is kept until the retention periods in Section 2.11 end. The configuration file request in Section 2.12 happens regardless of these choices. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing based on your consent before it was withdrawn.

6.2 Cloud Provider Access

You can disconnect any connected cloud account at any time from within the App or revoke access through the provider. When you remove a Google source in the App and no other source uses the same Google account, Fravora revokes its access with Google, deletes the stored credentials from your device, and deletes the cached media for that source. You can also revoke access at any time in your Google Account under Security > Third-party apps and services.

6.3 Location

You can revoke location permission at any time through your device's app permission settings. If weather remains on without location permission, the App uses approximate IP-based location through ipwho.is. Turn off weather in the App's settings to stop weather requests.

Photo GPS metadata is separate from your device's current location. Revoking device-location permission does not stop photo-coordinate lookups; use the metadata controls in Section 6.5.

6.4 Voice Control

You can disable voice control at any time from within the App's settings. When disabled, neither OpenWakeWord nor Google Speech Recognition will be active and no audio will be processed.

6.5 Photo Metadata Display

You can disable the metadata overlay at any time in the App's settings. Turning it off, or turning off both the location and landmark display options, prevents new photo-location and landmark resolver work when the App applies those settings. Turning off Enhanced landmark lookup disables the additional Overpass, Wikipedia, Geoapify, Nominatim and Wikidata lookups; Android Geocoder may still be used while Pro photo metadata and a location or landmark option remain enabled. These controls are separate from analytics and crash-report consent and Android device-location permission.

Disabling the overlay does not stop local EXIF extraction during media preparation and caching, erase existing metadata or geographic caches, or remove diagnostic logs. See Section 5.4 for local storage and deletion details and Section 2.8 before sharing logs.
