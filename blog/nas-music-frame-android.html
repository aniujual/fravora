Build a High-Performance Music Frame with NAS & Android

Published August 20, 2026 · 7 min read

While many users turn their Android tablets into digital photo frames, a growing number of enthusiasts are using them as dedicated NAS Music Streamers. With the release of Fravora v1.1.2, you can now sync your entire music library directly from a Synology, QNAP, or Windows network share with high-performance playback and advanced library management.

Why stream music from your NAS?

Cloud streaming services are convenient, but they often lack support for high-fidelity local files (FLAC, ALAC) or deep metadata customization. By using a NAS photo frame setup for audio, you keep your files private, avoid subscriptions, and leverage the storage you already own.

Key v1.1.2 Music Features for NAS Users

  • SMB/NAS Music Syncing: High-performance library syncing directly from network shares.
  • Hierarchical Playlists: Create complex, rule-based playlists that mirror your folder structure.
  • Sync Inspector: A new tree-view tool that helps you visualize exactly how your remote files map to your playlists.
  • Smart Re-linking: If your NAS IP changes or files move, Fravora automatically re-links tracks by relative path or content identity tokens.

Step-by-step NAS Music Setup

  • Connect your Synology or QNAP NAS in Settings → Sources using the SMB provider.
  • Go to Settings → Audio → Music and create a new playlist.
  • Use the Add Folder action and select your music root on the network share.
  • Open the Sync Inspector to verify the hierarchical mapping of your library.
  • Set the Scan Interval (PRO) so Fravora automatically discovers new albums added to your NAS.

This setup allows your Android device to act as a high-quality music hub that plays alongside your photo slideshow, complete with background playback and lockscreen controls.

Deduplication and Library Integrity

Fravora's deduplication system ensures that your library stays clean. It uses an identity token (normalized filename + size + tags) to prevent duplicate tracks, even if they exist in multiple folders on your NAS. On the Free tier, this is always active to keep things simple; PRO users can toggle it for specific playlist needs.
