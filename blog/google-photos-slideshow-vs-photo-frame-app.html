What Google Photos slideshow actually is

Google Photos offers a few slideshow-adjacent features: a Chromecast ambient mode, an Android screensaver option, and a basic in-app slideshow within the Photos app. These are convenience features — useful for quickly showing photos on a TV or during a party, but not designed for 24/7 dedicated frame operation.

They have no scheduling (the slideshow runs whenever you start it, not on a timer), no clock or weather overlays, no way to mix in photos from a NAS or local folder, and no outage recovery if internet drops. The photo selection is your entire Google Photos library or a specific album, but not a curated mix from multiple different sources.

The source problem

The biggest practical limitation of Google Photos' slideshow approach is that it only shows Google Photos. If you also keep photos on a home NAS, old photos in local folders, or a self-hosted Immich server, those photos simply do not appear. You either have to upload everything to Google Photos (which has storage limits and privacy implications) or accept that a significant portion of your photo library stays off the frame.

A dedicated frame app solves this by mixing sources. A single Fravora album can simultaneously pull from Google Photos picks, a NAS share, local device folders, and an Immich server. All sources rotate together in the same slideshow.

Scheduling and standby

A dedicated frame should wake up when you are home and go to sleep overnight. Google Photos slideshow has no scheduling — it runs until you stop it manually or the device sleeps. Fravora's running-hours system lets you set a start and end time for every day, with custom per-day schedules if needed. Outside those hours the screen shows a standby clock or goes fully black, and the device stays tappable to open the menu.

Always-on reliability

Google Photos' slideshow features are not designed to run continuously on a tablet mounted on a wall. They can be interrupted by notifications, system updates, battery saver modes, and other apps. A dedicated frame app is built around this use case — handling background keep-alive, outage recovery, memory pressure, and long-session stability explicitly.

When Google Photos slideshow is enough

  • You want to quickly show a photo album to guests on a TV via Chromecast
  • Your entire photo library is in Google Photos and you want a zero-config screensaver on a Pixel device
  • You do not need scheduling, overlays, or non-Google-Photos sources

When you need a dedicated app

  • You want a dedicated always-on frame that runs on a schedule
  • Your photos are on a NAS, local drive, Immich, or a mix of sources
  • You want a clock, weather, or photo metadata shown alongside photos
  • You want transition effects, cinematic motion, or voice control
  • You want to hide specific photos without deleting them
