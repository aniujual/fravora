Self-Hosted Photo Frame: Immich vs. Local SMB Compared

Published August 20, 2026 · 8 min read

Privacy-conscious users are increasingly moving away from cloud-locked photo frames like Nixplay or Aura in favor of self-hosted solutions. For Android tablet users, the two most popular ways to feed a frame are Immich (API-based) and Local SMB/NAS (file-based). Here is how they compare and which one fits your setup.

1. The Immich API Approach

Immich has quickly become the favorite open-source alternative to Google Photos. Fravora connects to your self-hosted Immich server using your URL and an API key. This approach is best if you already use Immich as your primary photo management tool.

  • Pros: Access to Immich's rich metadata, easy album-based selection, and high-performance loading over the API.
  • Cons: Requires an active Immich instance and a stable network connection to your server for initial syncs.

2. The Local SMB / NAS Approach

This is the "classic" way to run an offline photo frame. You share a folder on your Synology, QNAP, or Windows PC over the SMB protocol, and Fravora reads the files directly. This approach is best if your photos are just "files in folders" and you want maximum simplicity.

  • Pros: Works fully offline, no API management needed, and compatible with almost any networked storage.
  • Cons: Lacks the organizational features of a dedicated photo manager; you rely purely on folder structure.

Which one should you choose?

Choose Immich if: You want a Google Photos-like experience but on your own hardware. You enjoy using a web UI or mobile app to curate "Frame" albums that automatically sync to your device.

Choose SMB if: You just want to point Fravora at a /Photos folder on your NAS and let it run. It's the most resilient option for dedicated offline frames that might lose internet access for long periods.

Mixing the Best of Both Worlds

One of Fravora's unique strengths is that you don't have to choose. A single album in Fravora can combine an Immich album, an SMB folder from your NAS, and even a local "Favorites" folder from your tablet's SD card. They all rotate together in one seamless slideshow.
