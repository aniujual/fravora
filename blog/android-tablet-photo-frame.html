How to turn an Android tablet into a digital photo frame

Updated August 9, 2026 · 10 min read

Buying a dedicated digital photo frame means paying for locked-down hardware with limited source options and recurring subscription fees. An Android tablet you already own — or one you can pick up cheaply — gives you far more control: any photo source, any layout, scheduled playback, weather overlays, and no annual fee.

Which Android tablets work best

Almost any Android tablet running Android 8 or later works. For a dedicated always-on frame the key specs are screen quality and charging stability, not processing power. Good options across price ranges:

  • Amazon Fire tablets (HD 8, HD 10): Inexpensive, widely available, and supported via the Amazon Appstore. Good for fixed wall or shelf mounting. Fire OS works well for always-on use.
  • Samsung Galaxy Tab A series: Reliable build quality, good display brightness, and standard Android that supports the full Google ecosystem including Google Photos.
  • Lenovo Tab series: Competitive pricing with solid displays. Good for budget builds.
  • Old tablets you already own: A tablet that is too slow for general use often still has a perfectly capable display. Dedicated frame use is not demanding.

Hardware setup

  1. Choose your location first. Decide whether the tablet will sit on a shelf, stand on a desk, or mount on a wall. Route your power cable before committing to a position.
  2. Get a stand or mount. For shelf or desk: any tablet stand works. For walls: adhesive mounts or slim VESA adapters designed for tablets keep cables hidden.
  3. Use a permanent power source. The tablet should stay plugged in. Use a short, right-angle USB cable to keep the connection tidy and reduce stress on the port.
  4. Disable battery optimisation for the frame app. Go to Android Settings → Apps → Fravora → Battery → Unrestricted. This prevents the system from killing the app during idle periods.
  5. Disable screen timeout. The frame app handles screen-on state via its own Keep Screen Awake setting. You can also set the system display timeout to the maximum or use Developer Options to keep the screen awake while charging.

App setup in Fravora

When you first launch Fravora, the onboarding walks you through choosing a photo source — local folders, Google Photos, NAS, Immich, or any other supported type — and creating your first album. Your frame is already showing photos by the time onboarding finishes. After that, fine-tune the display settings:

  1. Enable Fullscreen Mode in Settings → Appearance to hide the status bar and navigation buttons for a clean display.
  2. Enable Keep Screen Awake in Settings → Appearance so the display stays on during slideshow hours.
  3. Set Running Hours in Settings → Slideshow so the frame wakes and sleeps on your schedule. Outside those hours the screen shows a standby clock or goes black.
  4. To add more photo sources or source types, go to Settings → Sources.

Choosing your photo sources

The right source depends on where your photos live. You can use any combination:

  • Local device folders: Copy photos to the tablet's storage or SD card and add those folders as sources. Completely offline, no accounts needed.
  • Google Photos: Use the official Google Photos Picker to select which photos appear on the frame. Photos are cached locally and display without a live internet connection once downloaded.
  • NAS / SMB network share: Connect directly to a Synology, QNAP, or Windows share on your home network. Photos stay on your NAS and are served over Wi-Fi.
  • OneDrive, Dropbox, or Immich: Pick specific cloud folders or Immich albums as sources. Useful if your photo library lives in another cloud service.

Display and overlay settings

Fravora gives you control over what appears on top of photos. Useful settings for a home display:

  • Clock overlay: Shows the current time. Position it in any corner at small, medium, or large size.
  • Weather overlay (PRO): Shows current temperature and conditions. Requires GPS permission or a manually set location.
  • Photo metadata (PRO): Shows when and where a photo was taken, with landmark name lookup for travel photos.
  • Display Mode: Choose Fit (full photo visible) or Fill (zoom to cover the full screen). In Fit mode, choose a backdrop style — Mirrored or Gradient — to fill the letterbox area.

Transition and motion effects (PRO)

The free tier uses a hard cut between photos. PRO adds ten transition styles including Crossfade, Slide, Cube, and Parallax, plus cinematic motion effects — Ken Burns pan-and-zoom, Drift, and Vignette Pulse — that keep static photos feeling alive. Transition speed is also configurable.

OLED burn-in protection

If your tablet has an OLED display, leaving static overlays in the same position 24/7 can cause image retention. Fravora's Anti-burn-in overlay effect (PRO) periodically micro-shifts overlay positions and varies their opacity. The Bouncing standby clock (PRO) prevents burn-in from a fixed clock position during off-hours.

Amazon Fire tablet notes

Fire tablets run Fire OS, a modified Android fork. Fravora is available on the Amazon Appstore and supports all core features on Fire OS. Google Photos requires signing into a Google account, which Fire tablets support via a standard browser login during the Google Photos Picker flow. NAS/SMB connections work normally over Wi-Fi. Voice control using wake-word detection also works on Fire tablets.

Keeping the frame running long-term

The most common failure mode for always-on frames is the app being killed by the system. To prevent this: grant the frame app unrestricted battery access, disable any power-saving modes that restrict background apps, and set Fravora as the device's home app if you want it to restart automatically after a reboot. Use the running-hours schedule to give the device rest periods, which also extends display life.
